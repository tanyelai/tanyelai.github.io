I am a visiting researcher at the Centre for Industrial Software, University of Southern Denmark, in the group of Serkan Ayvaz, where I work on explainability and the philosophy of machine learning. I studied computer science at Yıldız Technical University, then did a master's at Istanbul Technical University, where my thesis, supervised by Ilkay Oksuz, was on counterfactual explanation.

Everything I work on comes back to the same two things: what it takes to understand something, and what it takes to explain it to someone else. Cognitive science and the philosophy of language are where I go looking: what separates an explanation from a description, what it needs from its listener, and when it produces only the feeling of understanding. AI systems make the question practical, because they explain themselves now. What they hand back is an account, not a record, and an account need not be true of what it describes, or survive someone bending it, which makes it a matter of trust and security. They also take over the work that used to make a person good at it, and leave someone watching who still looks like the one who can, which is the same failure again: understanding leaves and the look of it stays.

At Promake I design and build the multi-agent stack. Before that I built the AI stack in hBreastMMG, Hevi AI's breast-cancer detection and diagnosis product for hospitals and part of the first radiology AI suite from Turkiye to be CE marked under the MDR. That is where the problem stopped being abstract for me: an explanation that a radiologist, say, will act on has to be more than convincing.
