I am a visiting researcher at the Centre for Industrial Software, University of Southern Denmark, in the group of Serkan Ayvaz, where I work on interpretable machine learning and language models. I hold an M.Sc. in Biomedical Engineering from Istanbul Technical University, where my thesis on counterfactual explanation for ECG was supervised by Ilkay Oksuz in computer engineering, and a B.Sc. in Computer Engineering from Yıldız Technical University. I went into a biomedical programme to see the data being made: how it is collected, where it breaks, and what the instruments actually give you. The degree says biomedical; the work in it was computer science.

My curiosity started with minds, not machines: perception, learning, and what cognitive capability is actually made of. I read the philosophy of language and human perception for their own sake and have never managed to stop there, because I trained in computer science and the artificial version of the same thing is sitting right in front of us. What holds my attention is where it ends up in our lives, more than any particular technique. That has led to interpretability where a model's workings can be read at all, counterfactual explanation where they cannot, and the harder question sitting under both: how you would ever tell a good explanation from a bad one. The material has changed several times and will change again, into theory, or something I have not met yet.

All of it has also had to work in production, which is a different kind of test. At present I am the architect of the agent stack at Promake; earlier I built the breast-cancer detection stack inside what became Turkiye's first MDR-certified medical AI product suite.

See also my full CV, the complete list of publications, my Google Scholar record, the questions I keep coming back to, and some notes on the non-technical half of this.
