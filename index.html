Hi, I'm Toygar, and welcome. There are a couple of ironies in my life. I'd like to slow down, yet my work, my field and most of my days move fast. I can't complain much, though, because learning and making things rarely feel like work to me, and my curiosity still keeps me up some nights. And then there's AI. I build it for a living, even though I believe the way we use it is making us think less. So I'm trying to keep my thinking and creativity my own, and not to rush past the beautiful things along the way.

Research is where I get to think about all this properly. I'm a visiting researcher at the Centre for Industrial Software at the University of Southern Denmark, in Serkan Ayvaz's group, working on explainability and the philosophy of machine learning. What I want to know is what it takes to understand something, and what it takes to explain it to someone else. When is an explanation more than a description? What does it need from the person listening? And why does it sometimes leave you with nothing but the feeling that you understood? I often look outside computer science for answers, in cognitive science and philosophy, especially the philosophy of language.

I studied computer engineering at Yıldız Technical University, but it was medicine that made these questions real for me. For my master's at Istanbul Technical University I worked in the PIMI Lab, where Ilkay Oksuz supervised my thesis. It used counterfactuals to explain how a model spots heart attacks in ECGs.

Yıldız Tech · 2018–2023
Istanbul Tech · 2023–2025

Alongside the master's I was at Hevi AI, building the AI stack for hBreastMMG, their breast-cancer detection and diagnosis product for hospitals. It's part of the first radiology AI suite from Turkiye to be CE marked under the MDR. That work changed what I ask of an explanation, because a radiologist about to act on one needs more than something convincing. These days I build multi-agent systems at Promake.

Systems like these now explain themselves. What they give you, though, is a story about what they did, not a record of it. A story doesn't have to be true, and it may not survive someone pushing on it, so this is about trust and security too. These systems also take over the very work that used to make people good at things. The person is still there, watching, and still looks like someone who could do the job. It's the same failure in a different form. The understanding leaves, and the look of it stays.
